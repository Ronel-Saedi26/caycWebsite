About

What is Youth Court?

Youth Court is a cost-effective community investment to reduce crime and build assets in our youth.

Youth Court diverts young offenders, referred by probation, law enforcement and schools, away from the formal juvenile justice system to a community-based system.

Student volunteers aged 12–18 are trained as advocates, bailiffs, jurors, jury forepersons, court clerks, attorneys and judges, and lead every part of the program. A youth charged with an offense, such as vandalism or possession of drugs, can avoid the pitfalls of juvenile court by choosing a trial before a jury of teen peers.

With a restorative justice and trauma-informed focus, volunteers help teen offenders be accountable, reflect on their choices, identify their strengths, and repair the harm done while restoring relationships with families, schools and communities.

Participants serve on a future jury, perform community service and may attend counseling. There is no criminal record after successful completion.

Why it works

A growing movement

Thousands of teens are active in dozens of Youth Courts statewide.

A jury of their peers

There is no right to a jury trial in the state juvenile system. Youth Court gives kids a trial by peer jurors.

Outcomes that reduce crime

A 7% rate of repeat offenders, compared with 70% recidivism in the adult prison population.

Accountability over detention

Civic engagement grows through accountability and education instead of detention.

Volunteers and Advocates

CAYC is a network of over 70 youth courts in California, committed to improving the lives of youth who have made a mistake that brought them to the juvenile justice system.

Taking a restorative justice focus, youth court volunteers help teen offenders be accountable for their actions, reflect on their choices, and commit to repairing the damage they may have caused. Rather than taking a punitive approach, youth courts restore family, school and community relationships. Youth courts train young volunteers to be jurors, attorneys, court staff, mentors, and case managers.

Adult volunteers assist with court proceedings, case management and mentoring. Advocates can provide meaningful community service and educational projects for youth, and offer speaking opportunities in schools, businesses and community groups.

Your gift of time helps provide counseling, academic tutoring and other services a youth might need. CAYC also trains schools and communities that want to start or expand a youth court, and holds an annual Youth Summit, at low or no cost, on topics like mediation, truancy and adolescent brain development.

How do I get referred to Youth Court?

All Youth Courts accept first-time, nonviolent offenders. Referral processes vary by community. A typical process looks like this.

  1. 1Youth are referred by school, juvenile probation or law enforcement.
  2. 2The respondent and a parent or guardian meet with a Youth Court representative to get program details and join the program.
  3. 3Youth Court volunteers connect with the new participant.
  4. 4The respondent attends Youth Court on the scheduled day and time for the hearing of the case.
  5. 5After the jury reaches a decision, the respondent is given the consequences decided by the jury.
  6. 6The respondent completes the restorative plan or dispositions assigned by their peers, including serving as a juror for other respondents.
  7. 7Once all consequences are completed, the case is closed and will not become a criminal record.

If the respondent does not complete the program satisfactorily, the case may be referred for formal juvenile justice proceedings.
